## 1. Executive summary

- **Niche chosen:** cinematic "what happens inside your body" documentary/explainer, long-form, faceless,
  produced with LTX-2.5 and MiniMax H3. Gen Z finance and AI-tool tutorials were rejected.
- **What wins (supported by 6 channels):** a **second-person story** ("you" are the protagonist) cut with
  the explanation, sold with **"What Happens To Your Body When You…"** and **"(Hour by Hour)"** titles.
- **What does not win on its own:** 3D visuals (accurate or AI), accuracy, video length, the niche itself,
  launch timing, or copying the look of a winner.
- **Our edge:** the **Explorer** turns the winners' spoken metaphors into places the viewer can fly into,
  plus **researched claims** and a **"What we simplified"** segment none of the competitors have.
- **Biggest risks:** inaccurate claims (policy + trust), templated AI output (inauthentic-content policy),
  slow start (first episodes may get very few views).
- **Next step:** produce Episode 1 — *"What Happens To Your Body When You Don't Sleep (Hour by Hour)"* —
  and judge episodes 1–6 on CTR (≥5%) and average view duration (≥40%), not raw views.

**Correction to earlier chat:** I previously said Think Science has the "best subscriber conversion".
With all channels in, views-per-subscriber does **not** separate winners from losers
(Think Science ~117, Digest 3D ~129, Inside Us ~141). The metric that separates them is
**recent reach** (recent median views vs channel size). The report uses that.

## 7. Deep dive: how the winning video is built

*Think Science — "What Happens To Your Body When You Eat Once A Day (Hour By Hour)", 12:10, ~1,900 words (~155 wpm). Structure only; the transcript itself is not reproduced.*

| Time | Section | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:57 | Hook | Reframe + master metaphor (warehouse) + big promise + "start the clock" |
| 0:57–4:00 | Hours 0–4, 4–12, 12 | One metaphor per checkpoint (valve, wallet, vault) |
| 4:00–5:20 | Hours 14–16 | Ketones, "the lift" |
| **5:20–6:47** | **Hours 16–18 — climax** | Warehouse metaphor paid off (~45% mark), longest section |
| 6:47–7:51 | Hours 20–22 | Objection answered before the viewer raises it |
| 7:51–8:36 | Hour 23 | "Dry sponge" metaphor |
| 8:36–10:15 | Practical section | Makes the video worth saving |
| 10:15–11:37 | Adaptation, caveats, disclaimer | Trust ("I want to be real with you") |
| 11:37–12:10 | Close + CTA | Emotional line + timeline-tied comment question |

**Retention devices:** open loop (metaphor at 0:14, payoff at 5:27) · on-screen clock · new metaphor every 60–90 s · objection pre-empted · second person throughout · practical section · comment prompt tied to content.
**Visual layer (observed by you):** a relatable person living the day (work, office closing, hunger) runs *in parallel* with the narration — see Section 9.

**Claim check summary (18 claims):** ✅ 5 correct (glycogen storage, hormone-sensitive lipase, ketones at ~8–12 h, fasting natriuresis, safety caveats) · ⚠️ 7 overstated (glycogen "critical lows", ketone "superfuel", "feel electric", autophagy timing from animal studies, GH "1,300–2,000%", insulin sensitivity "through the roof", salt "instantly") · ❌ 6 wrong (fat burning "biologically impossible" with insulin, "first time in weeks", three meals "clog", autophagy "never" on, "fountain of youth", muscle loss "a myth").
**Lesson:** the real mechanism skeleton makes it sound credible; the absolutes make it wrong. The honest version can be just as dramatic ("you already burn fat every night").

## 8. Claim validation — who checks, and how we will

| Who | What they do |
|---|---|
| YouTube | No pre-publish fact check; medical-misinformation policy enforced after reports/flags |
| YouTube health-source labels | Only licensed professionals or accredited organisations |
| **Us** | The only real check — and a competitive advantage |

**Two-layer sourcing**

| Layer | Sources | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Institutions / associations | NIH (NHLBI, NIA), CDC, WHO, AHA, AASM, Johns Hopkins, Mayo Clinic, NHS | Anchor: safety, recommendations, big picture |
| 2. Research | PubMed studies, Cochrane reviews | Mechanism details and timings |

**Wording follows evidence:** guideline/review → "research shows" · human RCT → "in a clinical trial" · observational → "people who… tend to" · animal/cell → "**in mice**, researchers found".

**Process per episode:** claim ledger (claim → source → evidence level → wording) → rewrite absolutes → sources + "what we simplified" in the description → never "approved/endorsed by" → optional credited MD/RD script review. AI (including me) can find sources but can mis-cite — every link is clicked and checked before publishing.

## 9. The two-layer story model (updated)

**Status:** originally inferred from one video (Think Science); now **confirmed across 2 winners**
(Think Science, The Infographics Show) and **absent in all 4 underperformers** (Nucleus, Inside Us,
BodyLogic 3D, Digest 3D).

### How the winning videos actually work

The narration and the visuals tell **two different stories at the same time**:

| Layer | What it carries | Example (Think Science) | Example (Infographics) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Narration** | The explanation, hooking in the first ~10 s | Insulin, glycogen, ketones | "…that is because…" |
| **Visuals** | A human story the viewer sees themselves in | A person working hard, office closing, hunger | "You already have X → you panic and do Y" |

- The narration explains the science; the person on screen **makes you care**.
- **The story doesn't delay the hook.** Words hook immediately while visuals build the story underneath — **in parallel**, never 60 s of story first.
- **Update:** both winners narrate the protagonist as **"you"**, not as a named character. The Host is a
  consistent *visual* person, but the narration always speaks to the viewer.

### Three roles

| Role | Who | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **The Host** | A relatable person living the situation — new per episode, **narrated as "you"** | Emotion, stakes, a visible clock ("it's 2 AM") |
| **The Explorer** | Recurring miniature guide inside the Host's body | Explanation and wonder — **our brand; no competitor has one** |
| **The Viewer** | Addressed as "you" | Sees themselves in the Host |

**The Explorer travels inside the Host** (the *Fantastic Voyage* structure). The trigger happens
**outside** (a deadline, a missed meal), the result shows up **outside** (a mistake, a crash), and the
cause plays out **inside**. **Update:** where competitors *describe* a metaphor ("warehouse"), the
Explorer *enters* it — the metaphor becomes a place.

### Rhythm per checkpoint: Outside → Inside → Outside

1. **Outside:** the Host at a moment in time ("Hour 24: you read the same email three times.")
2. **Inside:** the Explorer shows why (the adenosine "Pressure Tank" filling up)
3. **Outside:** the consequence ("You send it to the wrong person.")

### Visual rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Colour coding | **Outside** warm, realistic, cinematic · **Inside** cool, bioluminescent — no labels needed |
| Clock | Persistent on screen in both layers |
| Ratio | ~35% outside / ~65% inside |
| Host dialogue | **None** — narration only (avoids lip-sync, AI video's weakest area) |
| Numbers, labels, disclaimers | Added in the editor, never generated |
| **New:** surprise beat | At least one true, researched "shock" moment per episode — becomes the lead Short (BodyLogic's 147M hit had one) |

### Episode 1 — "What Happens To Your Body When You Don't Sleep (Hour by Hour)"

| Field | Episode 1 |
|---|---|
| **Host** | A 38-year-old project lead the night before a major product launch. Internal production name "Daniel" (for the reference sheet only) — **narrated as "you"** |
| **Want** | Deliver the launch without failing the team |
| **Trigger** | 11 PM: the build breaks. You make coffee and tell yourself you'll sleep after. |
| **Stakes** | Judgment, health — and at the climax, your life |
| **Outside beats** | Hour 16: sharp, confident → Hour 20: 3 AM, rereading the same lines → Hour 24: launch day, irritable, you forget a colleague's name → Hour 30: you laugh at nothing, then snap → Hour 36: the drive home |
| **Inside checkpoints** | Adenosine building up → caffeine blocking its receptors ("bouncers at the door") → the brain's cleaning system falling behind (mouse research — say so) → hunger hormones shifting (ghrelin up, leptin down → reaching for sugar) → microsleeps |
| **Main metaphor** | **"The Pressure Tank."** Adenosine fills it with every waking hour; caffeine holds the valve shut. The Explorer flies into the tank. |
| **Midpoint turn** | Hour 24: the caffeine stops working — the tank is too full to hold back |
| **Objection** | "But I've pulled all-nighters before and I was fine" → performance at ~17–19 h awake resembles legal-limit alcohol impairment (you don't feel it; you measure it) |
| **Climax** | Hour 36, driving home. Outside: your eyes close for 3 seconds at a traffic light. Inside: the Explorer watches parts of the brain **go offline while you're awake** ("local sleep", reported in research — verify wording in the claim ledger) |
| **Resolution** | A car horn. You pull over and sleep in the car. Narration: what happens to the tank during recovery sleep, then a brief look at the 264-hour (11-day) record from 1964 |
| **Surprise beat** | 17 hours awake ≈ alcohol-level impairment → lead Short |
| **What we simplified** | Adenosine shown as visible liquid; brain regions colour-coded; timings vary by person |
| **Callback** | Open: "You tell yourself you'll sleep after." Close: "Your body doesn't wait for 'after'." |

**Why it fits:** working-adult Host matches the 40+ audience; drowsy driving is a real public-safety message, not shock for its own sake; the story stops at a realistic 36 h while narration covers the extreme record; sleep is a gap Think Science hasn't covered and has proven curiosity (Infographics "Russian Sleep Experiment" 22M — a fictional legend; ours uses real science).

**Key sources to verify in the ledger:** NHLBI (7+ h for adults; effects of sleep deficiency) · Williamson & Feyer 2000 (sleep deprivation vs alcohol) · Spiegel et al. 2004 (ghrelin/leptin) · Xie et al. 2013 (glymphatic clearance, mice) · Randy Gardner 1964 record · CDC *Preventing Chronic Disease* 2023.

## 11. Verdict: the new channel plan

### Positioning

> **3D journeys inside your body — what really happens when you [do everyday things], hour by hour.**
> Explained From Within follows hidden processes from trigger to result. A miniature Explorer guides each
> Micro-Journey through cinematic 3D. The Explorer is fictional; visuals are AI-assisted; every episode
> is researched, lists its sources, and shows what was simplified.

Launch pillar: **Inside Your Body** only. Later: Inside Manufacturing → Inside Materials → Inside Technology.

### Format rules

| # | Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Second-person two-layer story (Outside ↔ Inside), hook in parallel | Think Science, Infographics vs 4 controls |
| 2 | Titles: "What Happens To Your Body When You…" + "(Hour by Hour / Day by Day)", or honest identity hooks | 3 channels, 6–340M examples |
| 3 | 10–13 min to start; test ~18–20 min once retention data exists | Think Science; Infographics trend; Inside Us shows length alone fails |
| 4 | Master metaphor set up in minute 1, paid off at ~45–55% (Explorer enters it) | Think Science structure |
| 5 | Objection at ~55%, practical section at ~70–85%, "What we simplified" + caveats, callback close | Think Science structure + our differentiator |
| 6 | 2–3 episodes per month | Think Science vs Digest 3D |
| 7 | 3–5 Shorts cut from each episode, led by a true surprise beat | BodyLogic lesson |
| 8 | ~1 in 5 topics evergreen/searchable | Nucleus |
| 9 | After 10+ episodes: a compilation of our own episodes with new framing | Infographics, Inside Us |

### Never do (anti-patterns)

- Single-food "eat X every day" topics; "miracle" / "N benefits" lists
- Fear clickbait ("before it's too late 😱", "stop eating X until you watch this")
- False authority ("Doctor Approved") or fake first-person ("I ate…") on a faceless channel
- 3–4 minute videos (neither Short nor story length)
- One repeated template, recycled topics, volume over quality
- Textbook framing ("What is [disease]?", "Anatomy of [organ]")
- Selling the tool ("3D visualisation") instead of the viewer ("your body")
- Copying competitors' claims or rewriting their scripts

| Metric | Target (episodes 1–6) | If below target |
|---|---|---|
| Click-through rate (CTR) | ≥ 5% | Fix titles/thumbnails |

| Views | Not judged before episode 6 | If CTR + retention hit targets but views lag → keep going (distribution lags) |

## 12. Risks, limits and open questions

| Risk / limit | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| 6 channels, observational — pattern, not proof | Our own episodes are the real test; track KPIs from episode 1 |
| Story and "you" framing always co-occur — can't tell which matters more | Treat as one package |
| Think Science is a direct, funded competitor | Differentiate: Explorer, sources, "what we simplified", topics they haven't covered |
| Inaccurate claims → policy strikes, lost trust | Claim ledger, two-layer sourcing, optional licensed reviewer |
| AI "slop" perception / inauthentic-content policy | No templates, human-written script, varied structure, disclosure |
| Production cost per minute of AI video | Start at 10–13 min; local LTX for B-roll; H3 only for character shots |
| Slow start | Pre-agreed decision rule (Section 11) |
| Audience is 40+ health-concerned | Cinematic, serious Explorer design — not cartoonish |
