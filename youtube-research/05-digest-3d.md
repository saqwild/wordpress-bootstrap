## Diagnosis — copied the surface, not the engine

| Winner's rule | Digest 3D did | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Identity / timeline topics (Think Science's best clusters) | **Single-food topics** — the cluster that flopped even on Think Science (eggs 85K, bread 10K) | Weak topics |
| Honest curiosity titles | Fear/clickbait ("Before It's Too Late 😱", "Stop Eating… Until You Watch This") | Low trust, likely low CTR |
| Truthful framing | **"Doctor Approved"** with no doctor; **"I Ate 3 Eggs…"** first-person claim on a faceless AI channel | Misleading — policy and trust risk |
| 10–15 min story episodes | Mostly 3–4 min | Too short for story, too long for Shorts |
| ~2.5 high-effort episodes / month | ~3 / week | Volume over quality → templated look |
| Positioning = "you + your body" | Positioning = "3D visualisation" | Sells the tool, not the viewer benefit |
| Clear series identity | Scattered: sinuses, fried rice, cheese, avocado | No reason to subscribe |

Unknown: whether their openings use a second-person story. If they do, story alone is not
sufficient — topic choice, titles, length and quality still decide.

## Lessons for "Explained From Within"

1. **Survivorship bias is real.** Think Science and Digest 3D launched the same month in the same niche.
   Timing and niche don't explain the gap — execution does.
2. Copying the *surface* (3D + health + clickbait) fails. Copy the *engine*: story, strong topic clusters, length, quality.
3. Never use false authority ("Doctor Approved") or fake first-person claims ("I ate…") on a faceless channel.
4. Fewer, better episodes beat volume.
5. Expect the first videos to get very few views; judge early episodes on CTR and retention, not views.
