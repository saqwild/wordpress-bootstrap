## Opening / storytelling (observed)

Second-person story, same pattern as Think Science:
"you already have X" → "you panic and do Y" → "that is because…" (explanation).
The viewer is the protagonist; explanation is attached to the story beats.

## Reading

**Isolates the story variable: story works without 3D.**
- 2D motion graphics, no 3D, yet the same second-person story structure as Think Science,
  and it sustains ~500K+ within the first week across unrelated topics (history, space, tech, body).
- A body topic ("What Nicotine … Actually Do to Your Body") performs in line with the channel (458K in 2 days).
- Title patterns recur: **"What Happens (to Your Body) When You…"** (27M, 19M) and
  **"(Minute by Minute)"** timeline (20M) — same as Think Science and Nucleus.
- **"Russian Sleep Experiment" 22M** — strong evidence of mass curiosity about sleep deprivation
  (supports Episode 1 topic). Note: that story is a fictional internet legend; our episode uses real science.
- Confounders: massive channel size and daily output — ratios are not comparable; use it for
  *pattern* evidence, not for growth benchmarks.

## Lessons for "Explained From Within"

1. The visual style (2D vs 3D) is not what wins — **the second-person story is**. 3D is our differentiator, not our engine.
2. Timeline titles ("minute by minute / hour by hour") work across genres.
3. Length is trending longer (15–45 min) as TV viewing grows — start at 10–13 min, test ~18–20 min once
   retention data exists. Don't jump to 40 min (AI clip cost scales with minutes).
4. Daily output is a studio model; Think Science proves ~2–3 high-quality episodes/month works for a small team.
